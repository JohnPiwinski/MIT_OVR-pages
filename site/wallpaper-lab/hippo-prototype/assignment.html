Why does this work in general?

The copies gD′ cover the plane with disjoint interiors, so D ∩ gD′ divides D into pieces. Because the domains are bounded and the group is discrete, only finitely many pieces have positive area. Applying g⁻¹ sends each piece into D′; the resulting pieces cover D′ without overlapping interiors. Shared boundaries may be assigned to either piece.

The outlined translation cell contains the same total area as several fundamental domains. Copies of the same color differ by a translation; their partial pieces together have the area of one domain. How many whole domain areas fit in one cell? This is the point-group order.
