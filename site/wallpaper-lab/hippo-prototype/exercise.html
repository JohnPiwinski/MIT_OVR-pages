Why the areas agree

Area of a fundamental domain = area of a translation cell ÷ order of the point group.

These areas use scan-coordinate units. The two domains have different perimeters, so they cannot be congruent.

Why does this work for any two fundamental domains?

Let D and D′ be bounded fundamental domains for the same symmetry group G. The copies gD′ cover the plane, so the intersections D ∩ gD′ cut D into pieces with disjoint interiors. Only finitely many meet D. Apply g⁻¹ to each piece: it lands inside D′, and the resulting pieces cover D′ without overlapping interiors. Shared boundaries can be assigned to either neighboring piece.
